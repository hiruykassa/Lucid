# STAR journal

Ten minutes, every Friday. One file per week, named for that Friday's date.

## Why this exists

Amazon's interview loop is heavily behavioral and Leadership Principle–based. In
February you will be asked for a time you dove deep on a problem, a time you were wrong,
a time you simplified instead of gold-plating. You will try to reconstruct those from
memory and they will come out vague, because the specific detail that makes a story land
— the number, the wrong hypothesis you held for two days, what you actually said — is
gone within a month.

This directory is the fix. It is the highest effort-to-payoff item in the whole project
and the one most likely to get skipped. By December it should hold roughly fourteen real
stories.

## Rules

- **Friday, before you stop.** Not Saturday, not "when I have something worth writing."
  A week where nothing went well is a story about a week where nothing went well.
- **Write down the numbers while you still have them.** "Latency got worse" is useless
  in February. "p95 went 1.9s → 4.2s after I bumped top-k from 5 to 20" is a story.
- **Include what you were wrong about.** These are the most useful entries and the ones
  you'll be least inclined to write. Being wrong and then measuring your way out of it
  is close to the ideal answer to half the behavioral questions you'll get.
- **Don't pre-package it into an LP.** Write what happened. Map it to a principle later,
  when you know which stories you actually have.

## Format

Copy `TEMPLATE.md`. Situation, Task, Action, Result — but write it as prose, not as four
labeled boxes. The labels are a checklist for making sure you covered the parts, not a
shape the story has to take when you tell it out loud.

The Result section needs a real outcome. "It went well" is not a result. What changed,
by how much, and how do you know?
