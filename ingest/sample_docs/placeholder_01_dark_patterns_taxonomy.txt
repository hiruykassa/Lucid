Title: A Taxonomy of Dark Patterns in User Interface Design (placeholder)

Dark patterns are user interface design choices that benefit an online service by
coercing, steering, or deceiving users into making decisions that, if fully informed
and capable of selecting alternatives, they might not make. This placeholder document
stands in for a real paper until the actual corpus is ingested.

Researchers have proposed several taxonomies of dark patterns, commonly organized
around mechanisms such as nagging, obstruction, sneaking, interface interference,
forced action, and social proof manipulation. Nagging repeatedly interrupts a user's
intended task flow with requests unrelated to that task. Obstruction makes a desired
action, such as canceling a subscription, unreasonably difficult compared to the
action the service prefers, such as signing up.

Interface interference manipulates the user interface to privilege certain actions
over others, for example through visual emphasis, pre-selected options, or
confirmshaming language that guilts the user for declining an offer. Sneaking
conceals, delays, or disguises information relevant to the user, such as hidden
costs added at checkout. Forced action requires the user to perform a tangential
action to complete their task, such as creating an account before viewing content.

This taxonomy is widely cited in subsequent empirical work measuring the prevalence
of dark patterns across e-commerce and mobile applications, and forms a common
vocabulary for the digital well-being literature more broadly.
