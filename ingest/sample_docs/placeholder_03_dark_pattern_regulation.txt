Title: Regulatory Responses to Dark Patterns: A Comparative Overview (placeholder)

This placeholder document stands in for a real paper on the regulatory landscape
around dark patterns, included so the ingestion pipeline has more than one short

Several jurisdictions have introduced rules that reference dark pattern practices
directly or indirectly. Consumer protection frameworks in some regions prohibit
interface designs that materially distort a consumer's ability to make a free and
informed choice, while data protection frameworks in others require that consent
mechanisms not be designed to nudge users toward disclosure through manipulative
defaults or asymmetric friction between accepting and declining.

Enforcement actions to date have most often targeted subscription cancellation
flows, cookie consent banners with asymmetric accept and reject options, and
countdown timers or low-stock indicators that misrepresent scarcity or urgency.
Legal scholars note that defining a dark pattern in enforceable terms remains
difficult, since many individual design choices are only manipulative in
combination or in context, rather than in isolation.
